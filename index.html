🛑 Sleeping HR ≠ Resting HR

Your smartwatch (Garmin, Fitbit, Apple Watch) records your sleeping HR during sleep — typically 10–30% lower than true resting HR due to overnight parasympathetic dominance. Using sleeping HR in HR zone formulas shifts all your zones too low, causing systematic under-training.

⚠️ Do NOT use the lowest number your watch shows overnight as your resting HR.

✅ How to measure true Resting HR

Sit quietly for 5 minutes (no coffee, no movement). Place two fingers on your wrist or neck. Count beats for 60 seconds. Do this first thing in the morning for 3 days and average the results. Typical healthy adults: 55–75 bpm.

📊 Max HR (Tanaka Formula)

True max HR requires a graded exercise test to exhaustion. The Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age) is the most validated age-based estimate (Tanaka et al., 2001). Click "Auto from age" below.
